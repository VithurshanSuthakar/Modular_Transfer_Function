05 / REAL INSTRUMENT

The Fast Auroral Imager.

On CASSIOPE/e-POP, a 5:1 fibre-optic taper mapped the f/4 lens to an effective f/0.8 image at the CCD. The NIR detector used 26 µm pixels.

Why the measurement matters

The ideal diffraction response at Nyquist is almost unity, but the measured NIR stripe contrast was approximately 0.35. Additional losses from lens performance, focus, fibre taper and sampling affect real images.

Ideal pixel response ~0.64
Measured stripe contrast ~0.35

The 0.35 value is an approximate bar-target measurement, not a directly interchangeable sinusoidal MTF measurement; pixel phase and target harmonics matter. Lecture slides 33–34.

06 / TRY IT YOURSELF

Design exercise.

From your lecture: a 2048 × 2048 camera has 5.5 µm pixels, 16 mm focal length, f/1.4 optics and λ = 600 nm.

Predict before revealing

Calculate the IFOV, full field of view, Q, and the ideal optics × pixel MTF at Nyquist. Would doubling aperture improve the image?

IFOV: ≈ 70.9 arcsec/pixel. Full FOV: ≈ 38.8° using 2 atan(Np/2f). Q: ≈ 2.68 (pixel-limited). Nyquist: ≈ 90.9 cycles/mm. Optical MTF: ≈ 0.90. Pixel MTF: ≈ 0.637. System: ≈ 0.57, ignoring other blur. Doubling the aperture at fixed focal length reduces the F-number and diffraction blur, but only modestly improves the Nyquist response because pixel integration still dominates; it also increases collected light.

Three useful takeaways

  1. Q identifies the relative scales of diffraction and pixels—not total image quality.
  2. At detector Nyquist, even ideal square pixels have an integration MTF of 2/π ≈ 0.64.
  3. MTFs multiply for linear shift-invariant blur stages; real optics and detectors must still be characterized.
